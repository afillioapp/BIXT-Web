# BX — website copy

## The name

**BX** is the logotype only. Said aloud it runs together as "Bixt", and that
is the name in prose: *Bixt reads it*, *Get Bixt*, *Just Bixt it*, *© Bixt*.

Three places keep the letters BX and must not be "corrected":
- the wordmark in the nav
- the favicon glyph
- the Drive folder chip, `BX - Northside Landscaping`, because the app really
  does name the folder with that prefix

## Voice guide

- **Never write BX in a sentence.** It is a mark, not a word. Write Bixt.
- **Real nouns only.** Receipt, folder, spreadsheet, your accountant, April. Never "solution", "platform", "seamless", "effortless", "AI-powered", "streamline", "empower", "unlock".
- **The outcome is the hero, not the technology.** Bixt reads the receipt. Never name the
  model and never write "AI": the reader cares that the shoebox is gone and that their
  accountant stopped chasing them, not what is doing the reading.
- **One idea per section, short declarative sentences.** If a line needs a comma splice to hold two thoughts, it is two lines. Hero headline stays under eight words.

## Titles — the rule

Every title on the site is **three words or fewer**, with one keyword wrapped
in `<span class="hl">` so it reads cyan. No em dashes anywhere on the page.
Body copy is one or two short sentences; if it needs a third, it is two
chapters.

## The story, in order

Nine sections, no eyebrows: the titles carry the sequence alone. The reader
goes problem, then what it costs to start, then the one thing they do, then
four beats where the system works and they do nothing.

### Two lines, measured

Chapter bodies use `.measure`, which is `30ch` and computes to **321px** at
1440, 768 and 390 (312px at 360). The band that renders as exactly two lines is
therefore **about 46 to 88 characters**: under ~45 it collapses to one, over
~88 it goes to three. The closing body uses `42ch` but is container-limited on
a phone, so its band is tighter, **66 to 85**. Every line above was measured at
1440, 768, 390 and 360 rather than counted by eye.

### Two things the copy must never say again

**"send it"** and **"shares it"**. Nothing is ever sent, shared, exported or
uploaded: the accountant already has read-only access to a folder in the
owner's own Drive. The hero said "snap it, send it" while chapter 8 closed on
"Nothing to send", which promised the exact thing the story spends eight beats
removing.

## FAQ

**Do I need an accountant to use this?**
No. If you don’t add one, BX just files everything neatly in your Drive and you keep it for yourself. You can add an accountant later, and they’ll see the whole history from before they were added.

**What if it reads the receipt wrong?**
You see what it read before it saves, and you can fix any field right there. Faded thermal paper and handwritten totals are the usual culprits, so it’s worth a two-second glance.

**Does my accountant need to sign up for anything?**
No. They get read-only access to a normal Google Drive folder using the email address you gave. They open it the same way they’d open any folder someone shared with them.

**What happens to my receipts if I stop paying?**
Nothing. They’re in your Google Drive, in your account. Cancelling BX doesn’t touch the folder, the photos, or the spreadsheet.

**Can I use it on a computer instead of my phone?**
Yes. It’s a website, so it works in any browser. Most people use their phone because that’s what’s in their hand when they’re handed a receipt.
